# VeriFact must build evidence ladders, not verdicts

Narrative intelligence is a young, loosely defined field: the term means a human cognitive capacity in AI research, a commercial monitoring discipline at vendors, and a military influence capability at US Special Operations Command, and no source gives a formal, shared definition of "storyline". The working consensus in practice is a pipeline of embeddings, clustering, LLM labelling, a risk or priority score, and an alert or brief. VeriFact should define a **storyline** as a time-bounded, truth-neutral cluster of posts and claims that recurs around one contested point about an event; a **claim** as an atomic, checkable assertion tied to a source span; and a **frame** as a rhetorical lens attached as an attribute, not a node. The research supports a demo that is **seeded-centroid storyline assignment, per-target LLM stance with visible uncertainty, observed-edge propagation, neutral duplication signals, and a descriptive before/after panel around Sarvam's response**. It does not support formal causal claims about that response, bot labels, or any published benchmark for Indic or Hinglish stance. The field's gap is also VeriFact's opening: no vendor page read publishes claim-by-claim evidence or a decomposable score, and no study measures how a company rebuttal changes narrative volume or stance causally. Several facts the project relies on conflict across sources or remain unverified (section 6), and the largest clock risk is the X recent-search window, which closes about **2026-11-23 13:37 UTC**, roughly three days after this report's date of 2026-11-20.

## The field uses four overlapping words for one stack

The sources disagree on basic terms. Blackbird's guide ([Blackbird guide](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/NI_Guide_for_Beginners.pdf)) makes a narrative atomic, defining it as "any assertion that shapes perception about a person, place or thing", and models actors, influencers, cohorts, trigger events and platforms around it. Larraz, Salaverría and Serrano-Puche instead build three layers, from individual false claims to a category to an underlying narrative (for example, climate measures as "an excuse to control what you eat"), and they borrow Fisher's coherence and fidelity as the narrative's properties ([Larraz et al., IJoC 2025](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/Larraz_GenAI_IJoC.pdf)). Kim treats narrative as a sequence of events with a character transition, split into content ("what is conveyed") and discourse ("how it is conveyed") ([Kim 2023](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/Fake_News_Detection_Narrative_Discourse.pdf)). Mandravickaitė models it as an entity-relation knowledge graph with a separate list of framing patterns such as binary us-versus-them and crisis portrayal ([Mandravickaitė, Text2Story 2025](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/paper15.pdf)). Mateas and Sengers warn that narrative is "a family resemblance concept" ([Mateas and Sengers 1999](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/Narrative_Intelligence.pdf)). The SemEval-2025 Task 10 taxonomy, a two-level narrative-to-subnarrative scheme that includes Hindi, is the closest community standard ([SemEval-2025 Task 10](https://propaganda.math.unipd.it/semeval2025task10/index.html)). No source defines "storyline" separately from "narrative".

Larraz's definition bakes falsity into "narrative", which would be wrong for VeriFact: its own seed storylines include defences of Sarvam and data-sovereignty arguments that are not false. Larraz himself cautions that categories built from false claims do not show the broad idea is false ([Larraz et al.](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/Larraz_GenAI_IJoC.pdf)). The recommended vocabulary therefore keeps Blackbird's "assertion" as VeriFact's claim, uses Larraz's middle layer as the storyline, and reserves an optional meta-narrative above it.

| VeriFact term | Recommended definition | Maps to | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claim | One atomic, checkable assertion, normalised, with the source item and quoted span | Blackbird "assertion"; Larraz claim; CheckThat! normalised claim | Evidence links, self-reported flag, status |
| Storyline | Time-bounded, truth-neutral cluster of posts and claims around one contested point of the event; membership versioned | Larraz category/narrative level; Gerard et al. story cluster | Centroid per time bucket, first-seen, platform lane, stance mix |
| Meta-narrative (optional) | Analyst-seeded grouping of storylines, such as "Is Sarvam really sovereign AI?" | Gerard et al. macro-cluster; SemEval narrative level | Hand-approved only |
| Frame | A rhetorical lens (sovereignty, crisis, authority, us-versus-them), stored as a tag | Mandravickaitė framing patterns | Tag on storyline or post, not a node |
| Stance | Support, contradict or neutral toward one claim, per item-target pair | SemEval-2016 style target stance | Evidence span, agreement-based confidence |

A tension in the project context file belongs here. It lists claim statuses "supported, contradicted, misleading, or unverified". Of these, "misleading" is a verdict category, and the evidence-not-verdicts principle argues for showing the evidence and stance counts and letting "unverified" and "conflicting reports" stand without a vendor-style label.

## Ten method families, each with a demo-or-later verdict

**Storyline discovery and drift.** The practical pattern is sentence embeddings, incremental clustering, then an LLM that labels clusters, with humans grouping clusters into higher-level narratives. Gerard and colleagues ran an online agglomerative clusterer over about 2M Telegram posts with a cosine threshold of 0.85 chosen against labelled pairs, then analyst-seeded macro-clusters that drift as seeds update; about 93% of the top-5 weekly trending clusters matched external events ([Gerard et al., AAAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.07684)). A 2026 study on roughly 17k Ukrainian documents per day replaced batch HDBSCAN with online DenStream, which scored best on silhouette (0.685 against 0.592) and human acceptance (0.84 against about 0.78), yet it explicitly does not model emerge, split, merge or fade, and it tuned its labelling on HDBSCAN output ([Vykhopen et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20680v2)). Larraz's HDBSCAN pipeline judged a cluster aligned when more than 70% of its claims matched one human category; 21 of 25 generated narratives (84%) were accurate, but only about 60% of records clustered correctly, 50 claims were orphaned, and the authors note that "tweaking some parameters significantly affects the final result" ([Larraz et al.](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/Larraz_GenAI_IJoC.pdf)). Online BERTopic is a poor fit: `partial_fit` tracks only the latest batch and cannot follow `fit` ([BERTopic docs mirror](https://huggingface.co/kisejin/TopicModelingRepo/blob/main/BERTopic/docs/getting_started/online/online.md)). Drift is tracked cheaply through per-bucket centroids and sizes; the verified literature offers nothing more elaborate. The sourced pattern, then, is seeded centroids plus an unassigned pool, which is a research-grade equivalent of Larraz's orphan bin.

**Claim extraction, matching and fact-check lookup.** CheckThat! 2025 defines claim normalisation (noisy post to concise core assertion) in 20 languages, with training data for Hindi, Marathi, Punjabi and Tamil ([CheckThat! 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14828v1)). In SemEval-2025 Task 7 on retrieval of previously fact-checked claims, top monolingual success@10 reached 0.9601, crosslingual peaked at 0.85875 (over 10 points lower), and translate-to-English with an English model matched multilingual models on original text; TIFIN India's recipe was translate, retrieve 50, LLM rerank, then reciprocal rank fusion ([SemEval-2025 Task 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.10740v1)). Google phased ClaimReview snippets out of Search in 2025, and the Fact Check Tools API page still shows v1alpha1 stamped "Last updated 2023-05-25", so its freshness is unknown ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/ifcn/2025/google-claimreview-fact-checks-snippets-removed/); [Fact Check Tools API](https://developers.google.com/fact-check/tools/api)). For a four-day-old business controversy, primary documents (Sarvam's blog and docs, labelled self-reported) are the evidence that matters, not prior fact-checks.

**Stance.** Zero-shot LLM stance is competitive with supervised models but inconsistent: Cruickshank and Ng's test of 10 open LLMs under 7 prompting schemes found no reliable edge over smaller supervised models, and a 2025 survey lists implicit stance, cultural bias and low-resource languages as open problems and notes that small phrasing changes can shift output ([Cruickshank and Ng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13734v2); [LLM stance survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08464v1)). No source reports Gemini stance accuracy, calibration, or any Hinglish stance benchmark. The defensible design is per (item, claim) stance, an evidence span, and agreement across two or three prompt paraphrases as confidence, validated against a hand-labelled gold set.

**Propagation, platform jumps and amplifiers.** Cascade metrics are practical only on observed edges. Goel and colleagues' structural virality (average pairwise path length in a diffusion tree) separates broadcast from chain spread, and SEISMIC predicts final reshare counts from early timing ([Goel et al.](https://www.5harad.com/papers/twiral.pdf); [SEISMIC](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02594v1)); both are classics the notes did not re-read in full. X retweets point to the root rather than the intermediary, so any X "tree" is a star plus reply and quote chains, and missing edges deflate virality. Reddit and Hacker News comment trees are complete, so they give the most honest numbers. Cross-platform jump evidence has three grades, strongest first: an explicit link edge, a shared URL or near-duplicate text, and embedding similarity with an earlier first-seen time. Only the first deserves an arrow. The 2025 cross-platform work uses platform-invariant discourse networks because direct edges are rarely observable, which supports that caution ([arXiv 2510.09464](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09464); abstract-level only). For amplifiers, DeVerna and colleagues found that a small set of superspreaders drives a disproportionate share of low-credibility spread, with pundits, outlets and affiliated personal accounts prominent ([OSoMe summary](https://osome.iu.edu/research/blog/identifying-and-characterizing-superspreaders-of-low-credibility-content-on-twitter)); their exact metrics were not verified. A transparent reach-times-engagement score plus PageRank on the quote and reply graph is a sound, explainable substitute.

**Neutral coordination signals.** Pacheco and colleagues build unsupervised coordination networks from shared behavioural traces, and the CooRnet approach flags repeated sharing of the same URL within an unusually short window ([Pacheco et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.05658); [Giglietto et al.](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1369118X.2020.1739732); window details from background knowledge). Bot classifiers are a poor basis: Rauchfleisch and Kaiser tested Botometer on 4,134 accounts, found imprecise scores, worse accuracy for non-English accounts, and thresholds that drift over time ([PMC7580919](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7580919)). That evidence backs VeriFact's no-bot-label rule and also matters for India, where non-English accounts are common.

**Risk scoring.** Blackbird names a Narrative Risk Score built from five signals (narratives, influence, networks, anomalous behaviour, cohorts) but discloses neither weights nor validation ([Blackbird platform](https://blackbird.ai/platform)). Alethea frames risk around Actors, Behaviors, Content and Targets without a named score ([Alethea](https://alethea.com/platform)). No peer-reviewed validated risk score was found. A decomposable "attention" score (velocity against the storyline's own baseline, acceleration, platform count and confirmed jumps, public amplifier reach, share of claims contradicted by evidence, a neutral duplication signal) differentiates VeriFact by showing every component with a click-through. The name avoids implying a verdict.

**Response and turning-point impact.** Slaughter and colleagues used per-post synthetic control on 40,078 posts and found Community Notes cut repost growth 46.1% but lifetime reposts only 11.6%; timing dominated, with notes at 23 to 47 hours cutting repost growth 38.8% and notes after 47 hours only 6.2% ([PNAS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12478135/); [arXiv 2502.13322](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13322v2)). That framing ("timing matters; growth effects are not total effects") is useful, but a platform note is a different treatment from a company's self-reported statement. CausalImpact needs a control series unaffected by the intervention and a stable pre-period relationship ([CausalImpact](https://google.github.io/CausalImpact/CausalImpact.html)), and neither holds here: only about 24 hourly pre-points exist and every storyline is plausibly touched by the response. The most defensible design is descriptive: hourly per-storyline volume, stance share and centroid drift, a decay-adjusted baseline, a changepoint check, and an addressed-versus-unaddressed storyline contrast (for example, the government-GPU storyline against the "Chinese models" storyline), labelled correlational. Offline changepoint methods are surveyed by Truong, Oudre and Vayatis, with penalty choice driving the result ([arXiv 1801.00718](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00718?context=cs)). I found no study that measures a company rebuttal's effect on narrative volume or stance causally, which is worth stating to the jury as a genuine literature gap. Coombs and Holladay's response-strategy ladder (denial through full apology) can label the type of Sarvam's statement once its form is confirmed ([Coombs and Holladay 2002](https://staticssl.sagepub.com/sites/default/files/Coombs.pdf)).

**Multilingual and Indic handling.** Hindi appears in SemEval Task 10 and CheckThat! normalisation; Hindi, Urdu and Bengali appear only in Task 7's crosslingual track. COMI-LINGUA has 125,615 Hindi-English code-mixed instances but no stance or sentiment task ([COMI-LINGUA](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21670v3)). `gemini-embedding-001` supports 100+ languages and `gemini-embedding-2` went GA on 22 April 2026 with up to 3,072 dimensions and an 8,192-token input limit; the Vertex docs now redirect to a "Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform" path ([Google blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/gemini-embedding-available-gemini-api/); [Embedding 2 docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini/embedding-2)). No Indic-specific quality figures for Gemini embeddings exist in the notes. Store language and a code-mixed flag per item, show the original text always, and include about 20 Hinglish items in the gold set so the methods panel can say "accuracy unknown" honestly.

**Temporal graph modelling and visualisation.** Spanner Graph is a property-graph view over ordinary tables, built with `CREATE PROPERTY GRAPH`, and has no built-in temporal semantics, so replay is a modelling choice ([schema overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/graph/schema-overview?authuser=7)). The recommended design is an append-only event log with `created_at` (platform time), `ingested_at` (our time) and `valid_to` on node and edge rows, storyline membership as its own timestamped edge, tombstones for deletions, and versioned clustering runs. This lets the replay filter `created_at <= t` and still show "what we knew at t". Change streams record inserts, updates and deletes with 1 to 30 days retention, but the docs do not mention graph tables ([Change streams](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/change-streams/index)). A graph-delta Pub/Sub message after each Spanner write is lower-risk for server-sent events. Sigma.js (WebGL) is the usual choice for large graphs and Cytoscape.js is richer for analysis ([Weber](https://weber-stephen.medium.com/the-best-libraries-and-methods-to-render-large-network-graphs-on-the-web-d122ece2f4dc)), and no verified node limits exist. Precomputed layout positions with new nodes pinned near their parent are design reasoning rather than sourced practice, as is precomputing replay frames from BigQuery so the slider cannot fail on stage.

**Evaluation and datasets.** None of the public datasets tests storyline formation around one live event. MultiClaim (about 28k posts in 27 languages, 206k fact-checks, 31k links) suits a claim-retrieval sanity check ([arXiv 2305.07991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07991v1)); MuMiN needs tweet rehydration, now costly ([arXiv 2202.11684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11684)). Topic-coherence scores such as NPMI correlate only weakly with LLM-based judgements and rank models inconsistently ([Yang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09008v1)). Kim's work is a warning: narrative features reached AUROC 0.899 on FakeNewsNet, but cross-dataset transfer fell to chance (0.465 to 0.493) ([Kim 2023](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/Fake_News_Detection_Narrative_Discourse.pdf)). The recommended package is a double-labelled gold set of 100 to 200 Sarvam items stratified by platform and by pre/post-response, with kappa, assignment accuracy, stance macro-F1 with bootstrap intervals, collection coverage, and a visible unassigned bucket. No source evaluates any method on Indian, multilingual cross-platform social data, so any VeriFact accuracy figure will be new.

Three observations follow. First, the attention-score and brief should answer the Gartner buyer framing of detecting before spread, aimed at communications leaders ([PeakMetrics, quoting Gartner](https://www.peakmetrics.com/insights/peakmetrics-recognized-as-a-pace-setter-in-the-2026-gartner-inaugural-emerging-market-quadrant-for-narrative-intelligence)), so the first-hour view after T0 deserves a screen. Second, the response operationalisation is the project's own proposal, not a published method: "dropping" is volume below a decay-adjusted forecast with unchanged stance; "doubling down" is sustained volume with a high share of posts contradicting the response; "reframing" is centroid drift plus assignment to a child or new claim, for example from "government GPUs" to who funded the private GPUs. Third, a published purity bar of 70% (Larraz) and a visible orphan bin give the jury a rigour signal in seconds. A knowledge-graph step in the style of Mandravickaitė (summary, REBEL triples, Wikidata validation) is a later feature, as her evaluation was manual and English-only ([Mandravickaitė](file:///home/claude/sources/pdfs/paper15.pdf)).

## Ethics and platform limits shape what the product may show

**Principles against the evidence.** Evidence-not-verdicts is supported by Larraz's caution on narrative categories and by the political climate: the FTC withdrew its investigation of NewsGuard on 17 April 2026 after opening it in May 2025 ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/ftc-drops-newsguard-probe-after-yearlong-dispute/)); Logically's technology and brand were sold out of administration on 7 July 2025 after Meta and TikTok had stopped working with it ([UKTN](https://www.uktech.news/ai/ai-fact-checker-logically-sold-off-in-administration-deal-20250707)); and in India the Supreme Court on 10 March 2026 agreed to hear the Centre's appeal on the struck-down fact-check unit but refused a stay ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/sc-to-hear-centre-s-plea-against-hc-order-striking-down-it-rules-126031001219_1.html)). Those cases make "who decides what is false" a live question for an Indian jury. The no-suppression principle has a concrete foil: the JSOU paper defines narrative intelligence as the ability to "understand, create, and manipulate narratives" and envisions limiting adversarial narratives ([JSOU paper](https://jsou.edu/Press/PublicationDashboard/256)), and Alethea markets an agentic takedown agent ([Alethea](https://alethea.com/platform)). VeriFact should offer no takedown or report-to-platform action, suggest evidence to cite rather than counter-narratives, and consider an audit log of exports. Blackbird's guide, by contrast, defines "manipulation" as the share coming from bots and trolls and describes amplification as "bot-like", which VeriFact should avoid by reporting only neutral wording such as "12 near-identical posts within 90 seconds; this can reflect organic copy-paste or coordination".

Self-reported labelling and conflict-of-interest disclosure have no published norm in narrative tools; the notes suggest adapting journalism disclosure norms. The seed case demands it: Sarvam's statements about private funding are self-reported, "privately procured" and "financed by private investors" are different claims, and a Sarvam investor's defence must carry the disclosure beside the quote. The public-figures-only rule has no direct backing in the seven project PDFs, none of which discusses profiling private individuals; AoIR's Internet Research Ethics 3.0 is the nearest framework but its public-data guidance sits in a PDF the notes did not read ([AoIR](https://aoir.org/ire30/)). The recommended mechanism is a config-set follower threshold, official-organisation and verified exceptions, and aggregation of everyone else into "N other accounts".

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is how little of VeriFact's differentiating promise is already solved in the literature: claim-level evidence per storyline, honest uncertainty on stance, explicit split and merge events, and a causal-aware but modest read of a company's response are all absent from the published and vendor material the notes could verify. That turns the research gaps into demo features. A visible unassigned bucket, a gold-set accuracy badge with a published 70% benchmark, an "observed edges only" label on virality, and a response panel marked descriptive are each cheap to build and tell a jury that VeriFact knows where its evidence stops.

The costs are asymmetric. The data that cannot be rebuilt is the T0 window on X, the original wording and timing of Sarvam's response, and manual LinkedIn captures, so those come before any modelling polish. The modelling choices are reversible and can use the simplest tools (seeded centroids, per-target stance with agreement, event-log replay), while the unresolved facts in section 6 should be verified by a single test call or page read each rather than by further desk research.
